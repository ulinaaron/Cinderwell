# Reference library

Use references for a specific lesson, not as visual templates to copy wholesale. Recheck a live site before relying on changeable implementation details.

## Visual storytelling and expressive composition

### Alpine resort concept

[Fixed-frame alpine resort composition](https://cdn.dribbble.com/userupload/45796526/file/fbc24ecbf8c4e830b1a41ec33756141d.png?resize=752x3153&vertical=center) demonstrates a contained document shell, a prominent conditions-and-planning layer, synchronized seasonal story copy with changing media, compact resort metrics, events, social storytelling, newsletter conversion, and a dense but recognizable footer. Use it when a destination site needs to feel like a curated field guide rather than a stack of generic campaign sections.

Do not copy the on-the-nose blue palette, tiny supporting type, or assume the concept image proves responsive or accessible behavior. A practical Cinderwell translation uses Hero, Card Grid for genuinely repeated planning paths, Content Slider for synchronized narrative and media, Events Upcoming Sessions, Loop with the people variation, Form with the newsletter presentation, CTA, and a child-theme fixed-shell and overlay-header recipe. Verify slider keyboard and non-JavaScript behavior, overlay contrast, sticky state, mobile image focus, and fixed-shell reflow.

### Construction concepts on Dribbble

- [High-converting construction website](https://dribbble.com/shots/27233523-High-Converting-Construction-Website-Design-for-More-Client-Lead): strong imagery that crosses into the interface, disciplined spacing, and subtle background texture.
- [Related construction composition](https://cdn.dribbble.com/userupload/48994703/file/95c8c5376d54c5b12f5fe1328936e58b.png?resize=752x&vertical=center): a contrasting hero followed by broad whitespace and well-separated sections. The hero image itself is not the lesson.
- [Warm editorial composition](https://cdn.dribbble.com/userupload/17397848/file/original-013e1166bfdfcb97946584973747941c.png?resize=1905x1429&vertical=center): emotive imagery and useful interface geometry create a welcoming tone.

Treat concept images as aesthetic evidence only. They do not demonstrate responsive behavior, semantics, or accessibility.

### Quality Edge

[Quality Edge](https://qualityedge.com/) uses product b-roll and imagery to show materials in context, then supports it with spacious content. Useful for strong brand presentation and visual product storytelling. Avoid copying overly thin type.

### NAVS and Slack

- [NAVS](https://navs.org/) demonstrates varied section types that accumulate into a sustained story.
- [Slack](https://slack.com/) demonstrates semantically organized product storytelling backed by strong creative resources.

Use these when a page needs narrative variety rather than a repeating card grid.

### WebAbility

[WebAbility](https://www.webability.io/) demonstrates accessible presentation enhanced by supporting motion and secondary sections. Motion must remain supplemental and respect reduced-motion preferences.

## Accessible content and bold clarity

### National Federation of the Blind

[National Federation of the Blind](https://nfb.org/) is a reference for contextual alternative text and content authored for screen-reader users. The key lesson is editorial: describe purpose and context, avoid filenames and redundant phrases, and distinguish informative from decorative images.

### Disability Rights Michigan

[Disability Rights Michigan](https://www.drmich.org/) demonstrates bold, readable type and a surface-level emphasis on accessible presentation. Verify detailed behavior independently.

### Twice

[Twice](https://smiletwice.com/) shows how a bold yellow-and-black palette, concise hierarchy, and readable product explanations can make high contrast part of the brand rather than an accommodation layer.

### BIGGBY Coffee

[BIGGBY Coffee](https://biggby.com/) is a reference for a clean, approachable consumer site with visible action areas, balanced imagery, compact section spacing, and intentionally contrasting text.

## Commerce

### Hygge & West

[Hygge & West](https://www.hyggeandwest.com/) demonstrates expressive commerce using dark text, light or pastel surfaces, consistent visual hierarchy, and predictable product browsing. Use it when rich imagery and pattern must coexist with clear product names, variants, prices, descriptions, and actions.

### Patagonia

[Patagonia](https://www.patagonia.com/) demonstrates how commerce, stories, activism, repair, and Worn Wear can share a coherent information architecture. Its navigation and dense, grouped footer are references for large content ecosystems. The lesson is structural consistency, not imitation of its brand.

## Product and SaaS

### Bonsai

[Bonsai](https://www.hellobonsai.com/) presents a feature-rich platform through customer outcomes and approachable illustrations. Use it for non-technical SaaS audiences, capability grouping, onboarding reassurance, and transparent pricing structures.

### WP Engine

[WP Engine](https://wpengine.com/) demonstrates spacious technical marketing: complex platform content, audience pathways, illustrations, product groupings, proof, and case studies remain scannable through strong section rhythm and whitespace. Use it to distinguish intentional breathing room from accidental empty gaps.

## Trust-sensitive services

### Pilot

[Pilot](https://www.pilot.com/) turns abstract financial services into recognizable customer questions, then supports claims with experts and problem, approach, and result stories. Use it for finance, legal, healthcare, and other categories where clarity and restraint communicate safety.

## Adding a reference

For every addition, record:

1. the distinct lesson;
2. the problem it helps solve;
3. what should not be copied;
4. accessibility or implementation risks;
5. the likely Cinderwell blocks or variation needs.

Do not add a reference that only repeats an existing lesson.
