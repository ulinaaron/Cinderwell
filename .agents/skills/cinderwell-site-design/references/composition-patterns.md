# Composition patterns

These are adaptable story structures, not fixed templates. Select and vary them according to the audience and content.

## Local or professional service

1. Outcome-led hero with real work, place, or people.
2. Three concise service or problem pathways.
3. Image-led explanation of the approach.
4. Process or expectations.
5. Local proof: project, testimonial, credentials, or service area.
6. Risk-reducing FAQ.
7. Contact or estimate CTA with phone alternative.

Use direct language and make calling or contacting easy without repeating the same CTA treatment in every section.

## Trust-sensitive service

1. State the customer anxiety or desired clarity.
2. Explain the service in plain language.
3. Introduce credible people or methodology.
4. Present recognizable scenarios.
5. Use problem, approach, and result case studies.
6. Explain pricing or engagement expectations.
7. Offer multiple next steps for different readiness levels.

Keep the visual system calm and precise. Specificity is more persuasive than exaggerated claims.

## Product or SaaS

1. Lead with the improved outcome.
2. Show the product in use or a coherent illustration of the workflow.
3. Group capabilities around customer jobs rather than internal feature taxonomy.
4. Add quantified or attributable proof.
5. Explain how major roles or audiences benefit.
6. Present integrations, onboarding, and pricing clearly.
7. Close with a low-friction action.

Do not open with an exhaustive feature grid.

## Content-rich organization

1. Clarify the current priority or mission.
2. Expose major audience pathways.
3. Mix current stories, evergreen resources, and actions using distinct but related treatments.
4. Use well-labeled navigation and facets to manage depth.
5. Preserve a single type and spacing system across commerce, editorial, events, and advocacy content.
6. Organize the footer as a compact information architecture.

## Commerce

1. Establish brand and product purpose visually.
2. Make category and product discovery obvious.
3. Keep product name, variation, price, description, and action in a predictable hierarchy.
4. Use editorial sections to show products in context.
5. Support decision-making with details, comparisons, reviews, and policies.
6. Keep shopping functionality accessible when imagery is unavailable.

## Page transitions

Use surface changes, dividers, imagery, or controlled geometry to mark narrative shifts. A transition must join the two sections cleanly at all breakpoints. Rounded or shaped section edges require explicit responsive inspection; they must not create unexplained gaps.
